## Intuition

A maturity model gives people a **shared vocabulary**: "we're at stage 2" is quicker than a 20-page assessment. That's the whole value, and also the trap.

Two shapes keep coming up:

| Shape | What it does | Good for | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Ladder** | ordered levels, each "more mature" | saying *where you are* | MIT CISR's four stages |
| **Map** | capabilities or perspectives, no ranking | deciding *what to fix next* | DORA's seven AI capabilities |

Three ways they mislead:

1. **Ladders imply everyone should climb.** A regulated firmware team may be right to stay at a "lower" level of agent autonomy.
2. **Vendor frameworks steer.** A cloud provider's adoption framework is excellent guidance *and* a path to its products. Read it for the steering.
3. **Survey segments are self-reported.** "X% of firms are at stage 3" usually means "X% of respondents said so".

## Smallest example

Ask of any model, in this order: *Who published it? What scope (organisation, engineering team, single agent)? Ladder or map? What evidence: research, survey, opinion?* Exercise 2 turns this into code.

## C++/Rust notes
- You already know this pattern: **CMMI** (a process ladder) and **SAE J3016 / NHTSA driving-automation levels** (an autonomy ladder). Both are useful vocabularies and both get over-applied. Shapiro's levels (L1.5) are explicitly modelled on the driving levels.
