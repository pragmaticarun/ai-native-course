## Intuition

These look at the **whole organisation**: strategy, data, governance, culture. They say little about how one engineering team should work.

## The models, as published

| Framework | Stages / structure | Caveat |
|---|---|---|
| **Gartner AI Maturity Model**, current wording on Gartner's assessment page | 5 stages: **Foundational → Emerging → Operational → Scaled → Transformational**, scored across seven pillars | Gartner's page blocks automated fetches; we read it through a web fetch. |
| **Gartner**, earlier widely cited wording (c. 2018–2020) | **Awareness → Active → Operational → Systemic → Transformational** | *Secondary source only*: the original research is paywalled. The names changed over time. |
| **MIT CISR Enterprise AI Maturity Model** (Weill, Woerner, Sebastian; Dec 2024) | **1 Experiment & Prepare** (28% of firms surveyed) → **2 Build Pilots & Capabilities** (34%) → **3 Develop AI Ways of Working** (31%) → **4 Become AI Future Ready** (7%) | Based on a 2022 survey of 721 companies. Stages 1–2 had below-average financial performance, stages 3–4 above-average: correlation, not causation. |
| **BCG, "The Widening AI Value Gap"** (Sep 2025) | **future-built** (5%), **scaling** (35%), laggards (60%) | Survey segments, not a design guide. We only saw the 60% laggard total on the BCG page itself. |
| **Accenture, "The Art of AI Maturity"** (2022) | Four archetypes on two axes: **AI Achievers, AI Builders, AI Innovators, AI Experimenters** | A 2×2 of archetypes rather than a strict ladder. We don't quote its percentages (not verified in the source text). |

<Aside type="note">
Percentages above are quoted only where we read them at the source. Treat them as describing survey respondents, not the world.
</Aside>

## Try it

Place your organisation on Gartner's current stages *and* MIT CISR's stages. Do the two placements tell the same story? Write one sentence on why or why not.

## C++/Rust notes
- Org ladders are like a product's *release-maturity* labels (alpha/beta/GA): helpful for planning conversations, useless for deciding which function to refactor today.
