Most "video understanding" is image understanding on frames you chose. The choice of frames is the biggest lever on cost, latency and quality, and it's yours, not the model's.

## Three sampling strategies

| Strategy | Frames for the 10.5 s test clip | Good for | Misses |
|---|---|---|---|
| uniform every 0.5 s | 22 | short clips, no structure | budget grows with duration |
| one keyframe per shot (scene cuts + 0.2 s) | 5 | edited video, highlights | events inside a long shot |
| budgeted (thin any list to N) | ≤ N | hard cost caps | whatever falls between picks |

`cost(frames, tokens_per_frame, usd_per_mtok, s_per_call)` makes the arithmetic explicit. **Plug in your provider's current numbers**: per-image token counts depend on resolution and the vendor's tiling rules, and they change. The course doesn't quote them.

## Audio and video as inputs

- **Cloud multimodal models** take images and often audio directly; for video, many APIs also accept sampled frames or a file. Read the current limits (duration, resolution, file size) in your provider's docs before you design around them.
- **On-device:** Gemma 4 E2B / E4B take text, image and audio through LiteRT-LM; video goes in **as sampled frames**, which is a design choice we make in the course, not a claim about native video support.
- Downscale before you send. Lower resolution means fewer tokens, but small text (scoreboards, UI labels) can become unreadable. M15 maps model coordinates back to full resolution for exactly this reason.

## C++/Rust notes
- Decode once, sample many: a C++/Rust frame sampler should seek by keyframe (`AVSEEK_FLAG_BACKWARD`) and decode forward, not decode the whole file. PyAV gives you the same in Python for frame-accurate work.
- Keep frames in GPU or shared memory between decoder and encoder if you can; copy only the few frames that go to the model (see the zero-copy note in [L14.3](/ai-native-course/on-device-media/m14/gstreamer/)).
