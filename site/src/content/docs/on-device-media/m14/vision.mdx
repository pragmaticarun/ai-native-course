Sending every frame to a VLM is the most expensive way to watch a video. Put cheap, deterministic stages first and let them decide what deserves the expensive call.

## The cascade

1. **Shot/scene detection** (ffmpeg `select=gt(scene,…)` here; PySceneDetect 0.7.3 as the dedicated library).
2. **Keyframe per shot**, slightly after the cut to avoid transition frames.
3. **Cheap filters**: drop near-black or near-white frames (ffmpeg `signalstats` YAVG), duplicates, frames without faces/objects (a MediaPipe Tasks detector), frames whose OCR text is empty when you need text.
4. **VLM** only on what survives: captioning, question answering, scoring.

<Snippet path="examples/python/m14/vision_cascade.py" />

On the 10.5 s highlight fixture, scene detection gives 5 keyframes; the luma filter drops the black shot; **4 frames** go to the (mock) VLM. Uniform sampling every 0.5 s would have sent 22.

<Aside type="caution" title="Cheap filters have false negatives">
A dark scene that matters (a night goal) dies at a luma threshold. Log what each stage dropped, and sample a few dropped frames into your eval set (L14.6).
</Aside>

MediaPipe Tasks (1.2.3) object/face detection and OCR are **not run** in the tests (*untested*); the luma filter stands in for "a cheap stage".

## C++/Rust notes
- Run detectors on downscaled frames in the decoder's pixel format (NV12/YUV) to skip a colour conversion; compute luma stats directly on the Y plane.
