## The kit

Ten generated "talks" (`recordings.json`): each chapter is a coloured slide with its own tone, and some chapters are separated by a silent black pause. The reference chapters and transcripts come from the same file. `keyframe_color` grabs one frame, scales it to 1×1 pixel and names the nearest colour, a stand-in for a VLM caption that still reads real pixels. `MockASR` records calls and peak concurrency, and can fail a segment once.

## M1–M2 · Segment and fan out

Scene cuts alone miss some boundaries: the cut from a black pause into a dark blue slide stays under ffmpeg's scene threshold of 0.3. The pause is silent, though, so the solution fuses the two signals: scene cuts plus silence edges, snapped to frames and deduplicated. Each segment becomes a `Send` to `analyse`; `max_concurrency` in the run config bounds the workers, and a `RetryPolicy` on the node absorbs a transient ASR error.

## M3–M4 · Merge, reel, verify

The supervisor drops pauses, titles each chapter from its transcript (a mock LLM), writes the summary and a word → chapter-start index. The reel takes the middle 1.2 s of the two longest chapters and calls the **P3 toolkit's `cut` tool over MCP**. `verify` re-measures: chapters monotonic, reel duration within 0.1 s, a cut within one frame of the shot boundary, loudness within 1 LU of −16 LUFS. Then `interrupt` shows the summary, chapter titles and any problems to a human; only `approve` publishes the chapters file.

## C++/Rust notes

- The fan-out is scatter/gather with a bounded worker pool; `operator.add` on `results` is the reducer that makes the gather order-independent (the merge sorts by start time).
- Sensor fusion in miniature: two weak detectors (video scene score, audio silence) beat either alone. Snapping to the frame grid is the same "quantise before comparing" trick as comparing floats with a tolerance.
- The verify node re-measures the output instead of trusting the tool's return value, like asserting post-conditions on a function you didn't write.

## Untested and not built

- No real ASR, VLM, OCR or LLM; no llama.cpp `mtmd` or whisper.cpp run; the cost and latency figures are placeholders, not measurements.
- No TypeScript port; no Temporal durable variant for multi-hour files.
- Only 6.8–11.6 s synthetic recordings were used, not real long talks.
